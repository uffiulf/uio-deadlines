DSO (Days Sales Outstanding)
Formel: (Kundefordringer / Kredittsalg) × 365

Hvor mange dager det i gjennomsnitt tar fra du sender fakturaen til kunden faktisk betaler.

⚠️ Hvorfor Falck bryr seg: Høy DSO er gründernes dødsfelle. Salget ditt ser bra ut på papiret, men pengene dine er låst hos kunden!
DIO: Days Inventory Outstanding ℹ️ Lager
45 dager
Hvor lenge deler/sensorer ligger på lager
Varelagerdager DIO
DIO (Days Inventory Outstanding)
Formel: (Varelager / COGS) × 365

Gjennomsnittlig antall dager råvarer og ferdigvarer ligger og støver ned på lageret før de blir solgt.

💡 Mål for gründeren: Få DIO så lav som mulig (Just-In-Time) for å unngå at tusenvis av kroner ligger bundet i komponenter.
DPO: Days Payable Outstanding ℹ️ Fabrikk
30 dager
Kredittid du får fra leverandøren
Leverandørgjeldsdager DPO
DPO (Days Payable Outstanding)
Formel: (Leverandørgjeld / Vareinnkjøp) × 365

Hvor mange dager du får lov til å vente med å betale fabrikken eller underleverandøren din.

🎯 Gründer-triks: Høy DPO fungerer som et gratis rentefritt lån fra leverandøren din!

Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)

Tidsrommet fra du betaler kontanter ut til fabrikken for råvarer, til du mottar kontanter inn fra kunden.

⭐ Målet: Få CCC så nær 0 som mulig (eller negativ, som Dell og Amazon). Da finansierer leverandørene og kundene veksten din!

Net Working Capital (NWC)

Differansen mellom kortsiktige eiendeler (bank, kundefordringer, varelager) og kortsiktig gjeld (regninger du skylder nå).

💰 Hva det betyr: Dette er den likvide kapitalen selskapet må ha i bakhånd for å sikre at hjulene går rundt fra dag til dag.
Current & Quick Ratio ℹ️ Likviditet
Current Ratio: 2.1x
Quick Ratio: 1.3x
Quick Ratio trekker fra varelageret fordi det tar tid å selge i en krise.
Likviditetsgrader CR & QR
Current Ratio vs. Quick Ratio (Acid Test)

• Current Ratio (Likviditetsgrad 1): Current Assets / Current Liabilities. Bør være over 1.5 - 2.0.
• Quick Ratio (Likviditetsgrad 2): (Current Assets - Varelager) / Current Liabilities.

🚨 Eksamenspoeng: Hvis Current Ratio er over 1.5, men Quick Ratio er under 0.8, er selskapet sårbart fordi pengene er låst i usolgte varer!

Case 1: Dual Price Shock (Sensitivity & Break-Even Analysis)

Break-Even & Leverage

A drone startup sells industrial units at 50,000 NOK (Price P) with direct variable costs of 20,000 NOK (VC/COGS) and fixed overhead of 1,500,000 NOK (Fixed Costs FC).
Due to aggressive foreign competition, they must cut prices by 10%. Simultaneously, global battery shortages raise variable costs by 25%.
Exam Question: By what percentage must the startup increase unit sales volume just to reach Break-Even now?

📐 STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION:
1. Baseline Situation:
• Original Contribution Margin: CM = 50,000 - 20,000 = 30,000 NOK.
• Original Break-Even Point: BEP = 1,500,000 / 30,000 = 50 drones.
2. After Dual Shock:
• New Price: P_new = 50,000 × 0.90 = 45,000 NOK.
• New Variable Cost: VC_new = 20,000 × 1.25 = 25,000 NOK.
• New Contribution Margin: CM_new = 45,000 - 25,000 = 20,000 NOK (collapsed by 33.3%!).
• New Break-Even Point: BEP_new = 1,500,000 / 20,000 = 75 drones.
👉 Conclusion: Sales volume must increase by (75 - 50) / 50 = +50.0% just to break even!

Case 2: Bankruptcy via Growth (Working Capital Gap Explosion)

Working Capital Gap

An IoT hardware startup starts January 1 with 1,000,000 NOK cash in bank. In January, they sign a massive B2B contract for 10 MNOK in revenue. The manufacturing cost is 6 MNOK, which the factory demands paid cash on Day 30 (Feb 1).
The corporate buyer has negotiated 90 days payment terms (DSO = 90 days).
Exam Question: How much external short-term liquidity (working capital loan / credit facility) must the founder secure before February 1 to avoid insolvency?

📐 STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION:
1. Cash Inflow (Penger inn):
• Day 1–89: 0 NOK (Customer cash arrives only on Day 90 / April 1).
2. Cash Outflow (Penger ut på Dag 30 / Feb 1):
• Factory payment due: -6,000,000 NOK.
3. Net Cash Position on Feb 1 without financing:
• Starting Cash: +1,000,000 NOK.
• Outflow: -6,000,000 NOK.
• Bank Balance: -5,000,000 NOK.
👉 Conclusion: The founder MUST secure at least 5,000,000 NOK in bridge financing or factoring immediately. Otherwise, the company defaults on Feb 1—despite recording 4 MNOK in accounting gross profit!

Case 3: Series A Valuation & Founder Cap Table

Venture Capital & Cap Table

Olav's startup reaches 10 MNOK in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR). A leading Nordic VC fund offers to invest 15 MNOK based on an EV/ARR valuation multiple of 4.5x (Pre-Money).
Exam Question: Calculate the Post-Money Valuation and Olav's retained equity percentage after the investment round.

📐 STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION:
1. Pre-Money Valuation:
• Pre-Money = ARR × Multiple = 10 MNOK × 4.5 = 45 MNOK.
2. Post-Money Valuation:
• Post-Money = Pre-Money + New Investment = 45 MNOK + 15 MNOK = 60 MNOK.
3. Equity Percentages:
• VC Equity % = New Investment / Post-Money = 15 MNOK / 60 MNOK = 25.0%.
• Olav's Remaining Equity % = 100% - 25% = 75.0%.
👉 Conclusion: Post-Money is 60 MNOK. Olav's 75% stake is worth 45 MNOK, backed by 15 MNOK in fresh growth capital!

❌ AMATEUR MISTAKE (Dividing by Pre-Money)
15 MNOK / 45 MNOK = 33.3%

Gives the investor 20 MNOK in value for only 15 MNOK invested! Never divide by Pre-Money.

✅ CORRECT FORMULA (Dividing by Post-Money)
15 MNOK / 60 MNOK = 25.0%

Equity ownership is ALWAYS calculated as a slice of the total enlarged Post-Money pie.

💡
Founder Takeaway: Dilution does NOT mean losing wealth. You gave away 25% of the shares, but your 75% stake is still worth exactly 45 MNOK. Crucially, your company now has 15 MNOK in liquid growth capital to scale hiring, expand marketing, and target a 200 MNOK valuation at Series B!

"Good morning. When founders approach their Series A, fear of dilution is often their primary psychological roadblock. Let's look at the financial mechanics:"

"Our software venture generates 10 MNOK in ARR. At an agreed industry multiple of 4.5x, our Pre-Money valuation is 45 MNOK. The VC injects 15 MNOK in cash. The common amateur pitfall is dividing 15 by 45, which would erroneously surrender 33.3%."

"The mathematically sound method is dividing the new investment by the Post-Money valuation of 60 MNOK. The investor receives exactly 25.0%. While the founder is diluted from 100% to 75%, their equity is still worth exactly 45 MNOK. Most importantly, the venture now holds 15 MNOK in liquidity to accelerate growth and position for a 200 MNOK Series B."
